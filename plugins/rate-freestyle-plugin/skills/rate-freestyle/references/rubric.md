# Lyrical scoring rubric

Use half-point scores from 0 to 10. Broad anchors: 0–2 weak or absent execution, 3–4 limited execution, 5–6 competent, 7–8 strong, 9–10 exceptional. Missing or unintelligible evidence is N/A, not evidence of low skill. A high score requires a reason grounded in the actual passage; profanity, fame, popularity, speed, and technical vocabulary do not earn points by themselves.

## Complexity

Assess rhyme architecture, internal and multisyllabic patterns, layered wordplay, semantic precision, and the integration of those devices with meaning. Distinguish phonetic recurrence from a genuinely sustained rhyme scheme. Distinguish a reference name from a developed allusion. Do not reward syllable density or obscure language when it reduces coherence.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Little effective rhyme or wordplay; devices are mostly incoherent or unsuccessful. |
| 3–4 | Predictable end rhymes and simple comparisons with limited variation. |
| 5–6 | Clear rhyming craft; some effective internal rhymes or wordplay, with uneven integration. |
| 7–8 | Sustained varied devices, precise language, and multiple effective relationships between sound and meaning. |
| 9–10 | Exceptionally integrated rhyme and semantic layers; rereading reveals additional supported meaning without requiring invented interpretations. |

## Depth

Assess specificity, emotional or conceptual insight, tension, perspective, imagery, and the development of implications. A humorous or boastful passage can have depth through precise observation and layered perspective. A serious subject is not automatically deep. Vulnerability earns credit for what the passage accomplishes, not merely for naming suffering.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Little discernible idea or perspective beyond filler. |
| 3–4 | Generic assertions and familiar images with limited specificity. |
| 5–6 | A clear idea or feeling with some concrete observation or developed implication. |
| 7–8 | Specific, layered insight or sustained emotional/conceptual tension. |
| 9–10 | Exceptional precision and development that produces an earned shift in understanding or perspective. |

## Connected development

This is a central criterion. Track the passage as a sequence: initial idea → developed relationship → consequence, reversal, or payoff. Identify what changes between the beginning and end. Reward semantic bridges, thematic accumulation, narrative causality, transformed callbacks, and setups that make later lines more meaningful.

A callback should do something new with an earlier idea. Repeating a word, keeping the same rhyme ending, listing unrelated references, or staying on a broad theme is not enough for a high score. Conversely, an unexpected turn can be connected when the semantic bridge is intelligible. Do not require linear storytelling from every style.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Lines mostly collide without an intelligible relationship. |
| 3–4 | Sound-based links or a broad topic provide continuity, but the ideas barely develop. |
| 5–6 | Several clear idea-to-idea transitions and at least one developed relationship. |
| 7–8 | Sustained development with meaningful transitions, callbacks, or a clear setup/payoff. |
| 9–10 | An exceptional chain in which later lines transform and deepen earlier ideas, producing an earned cumulative effect. |

Illustrative original example: a lost train ticket becomes a missed departure; the missed departure prompts a reconsidered destination; the closing line changes the meaning of "going home." The progression, not the train imagery by itself, is the evidence of development.

For a selected clip, distinguish development within the clip from a callback requiring earlier session context. Report both; do not silently credit an absent setup as if a new listener heard it.

## Accessibility when requested

Assess how readily a listener can follow the central idea, transitions, and payoff at normal speed, while allowing rewarding layers on repeat listening. Do not penalize dialect, cultural specificity, accent, or unfamiliarity to the evaluator. Explain a culturally specific reference before deciding whether it interrupts comprehension. With transcripts alone, label this textual accessibility; audibility and delivery remain unassessed.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | The central meaning cannot be followed despite adequate transcription/context. |
| 3–4 | Some meaning is available, but weak transitions or unresolved references obscure the point. |
| 5–6 | The main meaning is understandable, with occasional overloaded or unclear connections. |
| 7–8 | The idea and payoff land clearly while retaining some complexity. |
| 9–10 | Immediate clarity and memorable impact coexist with rewarding supported layers. |

"Availability" is not a standard substitute for this category. If the user uses it without a definition, clarify what they mean.

## Observational support for improvisation

Keep this separate from the lyrical total unless the user explicitly requests and defines another scoring scheme.

- **Not assessed:** Only lyrics or an excerpt without relevant session context is available.
- **Limited support:** Material is presented as a freestyle, but the available evidence does not establish spontaneity.
- **Moderate support:** Specific responses to live context, meaningful real-time repairs, or callbacks suggest spontaneous adaptation; preplanning remains possible.
- **Strong observational support:** Several independently observable responses to apparently unanticipated prompts or events occur in a continuous performance. This still does not establish that every line was newly invented.

Connected development is artistically valuable regardless of preparation. Explain what the observed connection shows and what it cannot establish.

## Confidence and verdicts

Separate transcription confidence, boundary/timing confidence, and performer-attribution confidence. A confident artistic judgment cannot repair uncertain source attribution.

A category reason should identify an actual device or progression and its effect, including a limitation when relevant. Avoid generic statements that could describe any clip. If transcription uncertainty changes the verdict, mark the round unresolved pending verification rather than forcing a winner.
